# Automatic payment-pause resume

## Why payment apps refuse, and when Detach must already be off

Google Pay India blocks payments when it finds an "unknown or unauthorised" app with an active accessibility service ([Google Pay help](https://support.google.com/pay/india/answer/17165523)). The likely mechanism is Play Integrity's app-access-risk verdict, which reports enabled control-capable accessibility services and excludes only Play-reviewed accessibility tools ([verdicts](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/verdicts)). A sideloaded Detach with `isAccessibilityTool="false"` always counts while its service is enabled. It cannot be hidden: the enabled-services setting and Play services' attestation see every enabled service, and declaring itself an accessibility tool would misrepresent Detach (it is not an assistive tool) and violate Play policy. Other UPI and bank apps vary; some reportedly refuse while *any* accessibility app is on.

Payment apps can check at launch, before their first window exists. Detach can only react to a window event, so a pause triggered by the UPI app itself is inherently late. Community testing also reports that newer Google Pay versions keep the block until the phone restarts once it has seen such a service, so after one failure every retry can fail even when Detach then pauses correctly. Detach therefore aims to be switched off **before** the payment app starts:

1. **Select the apps you pay from.** The selection (`Settings.paymentPackages`) is not limited to UPI and bank apps: an investing, broker or shopping app that launches Google Pay can be selected too. Opening it pauses Detach, so it is already out of `enabled_accessibility_services` when the checkout launches the UPI app, including the common case where a checkout SDK starts the UPI app by explicit package inside its own task.
2. **Pause first, or "Pause and open".** The authenticated "Pause for payment" control is available in Detach, in the status notification and as a **Quick Settings tile** (`PauseTileService`). The tile can only be bound by System UI (`BIND_QUICK_SETTINGS_TILE`) and only opens the same non-exported `PauseActivity` confirmation; it never pauses by itself. On the lock screen it asks Android to unlock first. For opening a UPI or bank app directly (no checkout app involved), `PauseActivity` lists the selected apps that have a launcher entry as **Pause and open <app>**. After the same confirmation it pauses, then polls `Settings.Secure.ENABLED_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICES` every 50 ms for at most two seconds and starts the app's plain launcher intent (new task, no data or extras) only once `io.github.detach` is no longer listed. If Detach is still listed after two seconds, the app is not opened, so it never runs its check against a Detach that is still on.
3. **Reactive fallback (late, best effort).** Opening a selected app straight from the launcher is detected from its own window, after its process and first activity already exist, so a launch-time check can still see Detach. An accessibility event alone is not evidence that the sender is in front: any app can send events from the background. Detach therefore pauses on a selected app's event only while Android reports no active window at all (the gap while a new app takes focus). Whenever another app is the active window, including the home screen, an unselected checkout app or Detach itself, it only re-checks the active package a few times (about 50 ms to 1 s) and pauses if the selected app's window takes focus. Package names only; no content is read. On the main thread, one active-window read per event is reused rather than repeated.
4. **Order of work when pausing.** The durable pending marker is committed, then the optional monitor start is attempted while the service is still bound (system-bound foreground-service allowance), then overlays are removed and `disableSelf()` is called. Alarm replacement, the paused notification and diagnostics follow after Detach is already off.

If Google Pay still shows "Payments blocked… restart your phone", restart once. Detach stays paused through the restart: the pending pause and its reminder are restored, but boot invalidates automatic authority, so resume by tapping **Resume** in Detach (while paused, the Payments section shows the one-line tip *GPay still blocked? Restart once.*). Another enabled accessibility app can cause the same block; Detach lists other enabled non-system accessibility apps by label in its Payments section (read from the component names in `enabled_accessibility_services`; nothing is stored) and links to Accessibility settings. It never switches other apps' services off.

**Not yet verified on a phone:** whether every OEM keeps a foreground-service process alive after its task is swiped away (stock Android does; some OEM battery managers may not, in which case Battery "Unrestricted" helps and the paused notification says detection stopped); how quickly `enabled_accessibility_services` drops Detach after `disableSelf()` on every OEM build; whether a UPI app launched inside a checkout's task always delivers an event to Detach (Android 14+ can hide views marked accessibility-data-sensitive from services that are not accessibility tools); and when exactly Google Pay requests its attestation. Selecting the app you pay from does not depend on any of these.

**Rejected: a `upi://pay` relay.** Detach does not register for UPI intents to pause and forward them. Checkout SDKs mostly launch the chosen UPI app by explicit package, so a relay would never be called; a relay would have to receive and forward UPI URIs containing amounts and payee addresses (against Detach's privacy constraints) and return the UPI app's result strings correctly; it would add an exported intent handler; and Detach would appear to the UPI app as the calling package, which may feed its risk checks.

## Modes and permissions

The default payment pause fully disables Detach's accessibility service and schedules one timer for the selected 1–60 minutes. No Usage Access monitor runs by default. The timer cannot know whether the bank is still foreground; it may re-enable protection while the payment app is still open, after which the service pauses again at once if a selected app is the active window (the payment app may notice the moment Detach was on). Choose enough time for the transaction, or resume manually when finished. The one thing the timer can see is whether the phone is in use: while the screen is off or the keyguard is locked, a due timer reschedules itself every 30 seconds instead of resuming, because a payment app left on its PIN or OTP screen checks again when the phone is unlocked, and Instagram/YouTube cannot be used while locked anyway. This wait is bounded: once less than 20 minutes of the pause's automatic authority (duration plus one hour) remain, the timer resumes even if the phone is locked.

Automatic enablement requires the owner's optional `WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS` grant. Installing Detach does not grant it. Without that grant the pause remains pending and the due notification/app explains the manual Accessibility Settings fallback; an automatic attempt does not authorize a Settings reconnect or claim that protection is running. An explicit Resume action can authorize one manual Settings reconnect for three minutes.

For manual recovery, open Detach, tap **Resume**, then use Android Settings → Accessibility → Downloaded/Installed apps → Detach → enable the service within three minutes. Names vary by manufacturer. Merely opening Accessibility Settings, opening Detach or tapping the ongoing monitor notification does not authorize a reconnect. If a request expires, return to Detach and explicitly tap Resume again.

Owners who want true automatic background resume can grant the optional development permission themselves from an authorized computer:

```sh
adb shell pm grant io.github.detach android.permission.WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS
```

This is a powerful Android development permission, not a normal runtime permission prompt. Detach uses it only to restore its own accessibility-service entry while preserving other entries. This implementation does not run the command, install anything, or change grants on the owner's device. If a grant is absent/revoked or a secure-settings write fails, use manual recovery rather than assuming protection has resumed.

**USB debugging and Developer options.** Some UPI and bank apps refuse to open or pay while USB debugging or Developer options are on. The grant is one-time: USB debugging is needed only while `adb` runs the command. `pm grant` stores the permission with Detach's package state, so it stays after USB debugging and Developer options are switched off and after restarts, until Detach is uninstalled or the grant is revoked (`pm revoke`). The grant surviving a restart does not mean a pause does: boot still invalidates the running pause's automatic authority, so that pause needs **Resume**. Detach's Payments section shows *Turn off Developer options before paying.* only while either is on (switching Developer options off also switches USB debugging off) (adding *The USB grant stays.* when the grant is present), reads `Settings.Global.DEVELOPMENT_SETTINGS_ENABLED` and `ADB_ENABLED` (read-only) to point out when either is on, and links to Developer options.

“Resume when Instagram or YouTube opens” is a separate, default-off consent setting. It additionally requires Usage Access and the secure-settings grant. Enable it from Detach's consent UI, then enable Usage Access for Detach in Android Settings. If the device has no package-specific Usage Access screen, open Settings → Special app access → Usage access and select Detach. Usage Access counts as granted when the `GET_USAGE_STATS` app op is allowed, or left at its default while `PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS` itself is granted (as `pm grant` leaves it).

The monitor only ever spends the pending pause's existing automatic authority (its token, wall/elapsed start, duration and boot count); nothing that starts it creates or extends authority. It is started when the pause begins, and **re-attached** to that same pause (same token, same deadline) when it is missing, opted in and all grants are present, from places where Android allows a foreground-service start: Detach's own screen while visible (opening the app, its notifications or the tile; only before the timer is due, so opening Detach never spends due timer authority), right after the owner turns the option on during a pause, the still-bound accessibility service when it refuses a reconnect, and the timer when it defers (best effort: Android 12+ normally refuses a foreground start from an inexact alarm, and the refusal is caught). It is never started after a reboot or an update: those invalidate the authority. The same-boot `BOOT_COMPLETED` that Android 15+ delivers when a force-stopped Detach starts again is not a reboot and re-attaches it like opening Detach does (before the timer is due only).

## Detection, battery and privacy limits

The optional monitor runs only during a valid pending payment pause. A dedicated `HandlerThread` queries recent Usage Events approximately every **two seconds**, only with an interactive screen, no keyguard lock and unlocked user storage. Queries cover at most the most recent **ten seconds**, never before this pause began; after the first query they normally cover only the time since the last check. A single event buffer is reused. No wake lock, network request, third-party runtime dependency, content/class-name inspection or usage-history persistence is used. The app still requests neither `INTERNET` nor `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES`.

This is near-immediate **best effort**, not instantaneous detection or a hard real-time upper bound: ordinary detection is within approximately one polling interval **after Android makes the event available**, plus binder/scheduler delay. Usage Events are not a topmost-window or payment-completion API; OEM behavior, delayed/missing events, multi-window and process termination can delay or prevent detection. There are no device measurements proving a battery/RAM improvement or a particular battery cost.

Only package names, foreground/background event types and timestamps are processed. Recent foreground and unresolved selected-app package hints are held in memory, not written as a history. A selected-app hint (UPI, bank or an app you pay from) takes priority over a social hint. A selected app observed moving to the foreground lasts until Android reports that it moved to the background, so sitting on one payment or PIN screen does not look like "finished"; it is bounded by **ten minutes** without a new event. The app that started the pause predates the monitor's query window, so it is pre-filled: held the same way only if the pause came from its own active window (or a startup re-check that read it), and otherwise (a pause from an event alone, or "Pause and open" before the app has started) only as a 30-second hint until a real foreground event arrives. This is safe only because observation must be continuous: screen lock/off or a query gap longer than ten seconds discards all cached state. Consecutive queries overlap by five seconds, because Android timestamps an event when it is reported but stores it slightly later, so an event stamped just before one query can first appear in the next; events already applied are skipped by package, type and time, and an older event for a package never overrides a newer one already applied. After an unlock (or monitor start, or a gap), a due timer waits until observation has run for one full poll, so a payment app coming back to the front on unlock is seen before the timer may resume; during those first two polls the timer-side check also counts as "payment known". An Instagram/YouTube foreground observed after the pause began now lasts until Android reports that app moved to the background (or observation is interrupted, which clears everything), not 30 seconds: both are effectively single-activity apps, so scrolling produces no new events, and a payment hold that clears late (its background event arrives after the owner already switched to Instagram) must still find Instagram in front. Every poll re-evaluates, so protection resumes on the first poll after payment evidence clears. Missing a social background event can only cause a resume while the owner is somewhere else, never while a selected app is in front, because payment evidence is checked first. The starting app pre-filled from the active window is held exactly like a selected app observed in front: only its own background event or the ten-minute bound releases it, never an Instagram/YouTube foreground, because in split screen the payment app stays resumed and on screen next to Instagram. If a hold ends by the ten-minute bound instead of a background event, an Instagram/YouTube foreground seen while it was held no longer counts; only a fresh one does. A re-attached monitor pre-fills the starting app too, also when re-attached from Detach's own screen: in split screen that app can still be in front next to Detach. When the monitor restarts or is re-attached well after the pause began, its first query also reads the starting app's own events back to the pause start (bounded by ten minutes, that package only), so a payment app that already left is released by its real background event instead of holding the pause. Split screen with a selected app keeps the pause (both apps are reported in front); that is intentional. The timer-side payment hint is additionally accepted only if a query completed in the last four seconds, and each deferral is 30 seconds within the pause's bounded automatic authority (duration plus one hour). Once a hint is gone, foreground is **unknown**, not “banking finished”; the timer may resume, and the service then pauses again at once if a selected app is still the active window (see below). Do not rely on this monitor to establish transaction completion.

The foreground service has a private ongoing notification, an explicit immutable tap into Detach, no binder interface and no exported component. The public lockscreen version has no package information. Both notification versions request `localOnly`, an advisory bridge/companion-device hint, not an absolute guarantee against forwarding by privileged/system notification listeners. Disabling the feature, revoking Usage Access, ending a pause or completing a resume stops it. Screen-off/lock removes polling rather than scheduling repeated checks; screen-on and unlock broadcasts restart it, and if the keyguard still reads as locked when they arrive (unlock animation, face unlock, Smart Lock), three bounded re-checks at 0.25, 1 and 3 seconds follow, only while the screen is on. These broadcasts also re-check the pause deadline, which the uptime-based expiry cannot see while asleep. An AppOps listener observes Usage Access revocation even while screen-off.

**Lifecycle (fixed in this release).** On a Motorola phone running Android 17 with 0.5.0, the monitor started correctly and was stopped ten seconds later when the owner swiped Detach out of Recents (typically "Clear all" while closing the UPI app): the service declared `stopWithTask="true"`, so Android stopped it, and with no foreground service left the process was killed for the removed task. Nothing restarted it, so Instagram/YouTube could never resume protection; only the timer was left. Now:

- `stopWithTask="false"`: removing any Detach task leaves the monitor running, and `onTaskRemoved` only re-checks eligibility. Stock Android does not kill a process with a foreground service for a removed task.
- Sticky: if Android kills the process anyway, it restarts the service with a null intent (only Android can deliver one; the service is not exported). The restart monitors only if the pending pause, its automatic authority, the opt-in and both grants are all still valid, and otherwise stops at once. Android 12+ may refuse the restarted service's foreground start from the background (unless Detach is exempt, for example battery "Unrestricted"); it then stops cleanly. `START_REDELIVER_INTENT` is not used: Android delays redelivery by twice the time the intent was being processed, which for a long-running service means minutes.
- Every start calls `startForeground()` first and only then validates: a `startForegroundService()` start that is stopped before `startForeground()` makes Android crash the process. Rejected starts remove the notification and call `stopSelf(startId)`, so a newer start on its way is never torn down; a stale start never stops a monitor that is serving the current pause. `Pause.stop` does not call `stopService()` while an accepted start has not arrived yet, for the same reason.
- The notification uses `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_IMMEDIATE` (Android 12+). The old 10-second display deferral raced the stop on the phone and left a monitor notification behind with no service. Whenever Detach code runs (status refresh, timer, app open, service connect), a monitor notification without a running monitor is cancelled; if Android still marks it as a foreground-service notification (which an app cannot cancel), Detach's visible screen starts the service without a token, which replaces the notification and stops at once.
- Only the monitor's own notification says detection is on, because it disappears with the service. The paused status notification cannot be updated once the process is gone, so it never claims detection is running; when Detach code runs and finds the monitor missing it says **Detection stopped · resumes by about HH:MM** (the same time the screen shows; tapping it opens Detach, which restarts detection). If the whole app is stopped and nothing of Detach runs again, that text does not appear until the timer or the owner opens Detach. Detach's screen and the notification take their paused text from the same status (`PauseStatus`: reconnecting, due, detection on/stopped, timer-only automatic, manual, needs USB), and the screen re-renders when it changes (status refreshes, the service connecting or disconnecting, and a settings observer on `enabled_accessibility_services`), without polling. The notification is posted only when its text changes, as an update of the same notification id with `setOnlyAlertOnce`; an unchanged refresh neither cancels nor re-posts it.
- **Force-stop.** Android removes a force-stopped app's alarms and notifications (Android 15+ also its PendingIntents; Android 9 to 14 keep them) and runs nothing of it until it is started again, so during that time the pause is not visible and nothing can resume it. Opening Detach, `PauseActivity` (tile) or the tile's `onStartListening` runs `Pause.resumeIfDue`: a future deadline gets its alarm again; a due timer with intact automatic authority keeps the alarm Detach last set: one still approaching a later time is set again for that same time, one inside Android's delivery window is left alone, and one that is gone or overdue past Android's latest delivery (plus five seconds) is set again for 30 seconds later (an undelivered alarm is never moved later). Whether an alarm is outstanding comes from a record Detach keeps when it sets one (target time, Android's latest delivery in elapsed time, boot count; cleared on delivery and cancel), not from whether its PendingIntent exists: that survives a force-stop on Android 9 to 14 and outlives every delivered alarm; the status notification is posted; and before the timer is due app detection is re-attached to the same pause. Android 15+ also delivers `BOOT_COMPLETED` (after `PACKAGE_UNSTOPPED`) when a stopped app starts again, in the same boot; Detach treats that as a re-arm, not a reboot (see Authority below).
- **Stuck reconnect.** On the Motorola (Android 17), Detach's process was killed while Android was binding the service after a resume ("app is cached, not scheduling restart of crashed service"). Android then kept Detach in `enabled_accessibility_services` and in its "binding services" set with no service record, so nothing ever connected, and writing the same list again changed nothing. When Detach is listed but `DetachService` is not running in its process, `Pause.resume` (explicit tap, timer or app detection) now removes only Detach's own entry and, after one second, writes the original list back (or appends Detach if something else changed the list meanwhile), keeping `accessibility_enabled=1`; removing and re-adding the entry was confirmed on the phone to make Android bind it again. The gap matters: two writes in a row can be read by the accessibility manager as no change. After every enable request Detach checks once more after five seconds and forces at most two rebinds per request, only while that request is still valid; Detach's visible screen does the same single check when it shows the waiting state. Before taking the entry out Detach writes a durable "rebind in progress" marker; the timer's receiver keeps its broadcast open (`goAsync`) until the entry is back, so the process is not cached and killed in that second. If the process dies in the gap anyway, the next run of Detach (opening it, the tile, the timer, boot) puts the entry back, but only under the same rule as the normal second half: no pause pending, or a valid request still waiting, and for a same-boot marker at most three minutes old. A pause recorded during the gap (a bind already on its way connected, used the request and found a selected payment app in front) removes the request, so Detach is then not put back while that app is in front. While a rebind is in progress the status is "reconnecting", not what the gap looks like. If Detach stays listed but unconnected after the request has expired, nothing retries: the notification says **Didn't resume · tap to retry** and the screen *Didn't resume. Tap Resume to retry.* (one status refresh is scheduled for the moment a request expires; while a request waits the notification already says to open Detach if protection doesn't come back, in case the process dies). Authorization is unchanged: the reconnect still has to consume the single-use, three-minute request, or it switches itself off again. A due timer whose earlier request expired while Detach stayed listed (and not running) may now request a reconnect itself; a listed Detach with a still-valid request, or a running service, still means a reconnect is already on its way.
- `Pause.resume` no longer stops the monitor before the reconnect is authorized and requested, so a manual Resume whose authorization cannot be recorded leaves it running for the still-valid pause. It stops after a successful enable, after a failed enable (that ends the pause), after a failed commit on the automatic path (whose authority is already spent), and again when the service reports that it started.

Android's "Active apps" (foreground-service task manager) can still stop the whole app; that is the owner's choice and the timer/manual fallback remains.

## Authority and fail-closed behavior

Starting a pause must successfully commit its durable pending marker **before** the parent accessibility service disables itself. `Pause.begin(...)` returns false on failure; callers must leave protection running in that case.

Automatic authority is bound to a unique pending pause, the original wall and elapsed start times, duration and boot count. Wall/elapsed ages must agree within five seconds. Timer authority is valid only at/after both original deadlines and for at most one additional hour; it is not renewed by a late delivery, app lifecycle callback, notification tap, boot or update. A reboot and an update explicitly invalidate it (token and any waiting reconnect request are removed). `BOOT_COMPLETED` counts as a reboot only if `Settings.Global.BOOT_COUNT` differs from the count stored with the pause (or cannot be read) or the elapsed clock is behind the pause's elapsed start; Android 15+ re-delivers `BOOT_COMPLETED` to a force-stopped app when it is next started in the same boot, and that only restores the alarm, the status notification and (before the timer is due) app detection. Nothing is created or extended either way, and a real reboot would also fail the boot-count checks of the token and the reconnect request on its own. A stale, malformed or mismatched marker remains paused with manual fallback. An automatic attempt is single-use. A successful secure-settings write retains the pending marker until the accessibility service consumes a separate wall/elapsed/boot-bound, single-use reconnect authorization and reports that it started. The legacy `ACTION_RESUME` broadcast remains ignored.

Rendering reminders must not cancel an authorized due alarm that Android has not delivered yet. App opening/UI countdown refresh may restore the original future alarm but cannot invent a new deadline or automatic token. If a force-stop removed a due timer's alarm (or it is overdue past Android's latest delivery), opening Detach sets it again 30 seconds later; when it fires it goes through the same lock, payment and authority checks as the original, and authority still ends at the original bound. A fresh known-payment hint at delivery may defer the single outstanding alarm by 30 seconds, preserving timer fallback if the monitor is then killed. This does not change the original pause deadline or extend authority; invalid/stale authority still requires manual fallback.

**Right after a resume for Instagram/YouTube.** The active window can briefly still be the payment app the owner just left. For about 0.4 seconds after such a reconnect, a selected app as the active window or as an event sender does not pause by itself; package-only re-checks at about 0.4, 0.7 and 1 second decide, and a selected app really in front still pauses within that second. For the first second after any reconnect, an event from a selected app while Android reports no active window (usually the exit transition of the app just left) only triggers those re-checks instead of pausing at once. Without this, a stale read re-paused Detach with a new start time, and the new monitor could not see the Instagram window that was already in front.

**No exemption for a selected app in front.** There is no post-resume exemption: if a timer or monitor resume reconnects the service while a selected app is the active window, the startup check pauses again immediately, and because the active window is often unavailable right after binding, the service re-checks the active package at about 50, 150, 300, 600 and 1000 ms. Earlier previews suppressed the original bank for three minutes after a timer resume to avoid a "loop"; that let Detach stay enabled inside the payment app on retry, so it was removed (stale suppression keys are ignored and deleted). No suppression is needed against a tight enable/disable loop: every reconnect needs fresh authority (a timer due at least one pause length after the new pause began, a newly observed Instagram/YouTube foreground, or an explicit Resume tap), and a selected app's foreground never grants it. In timer-only mode a resume over a selected app still re-enables Detach for a moment before it pauses again, which a payment app could notice (Google Pay may then block until a restart); choose enough time or enable app detection. Only app detection keeps the timer waiting while a selected app is in front, and the app's Payments copy says so only in that mode. No package learned from Usage Events is persisted by the monitor.

## Android 12–15 foreground-service constraints

The monitor is a narrowly declared `specialUse` foreground service with `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` and `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE`, plus its manifest subtype explanation. It does not impersonate a media, location, health or data-sync service. Android 14 requires the declared type/permission. `specialUse` has no runtime prerequisites of its own, but does not bypass ordinary startup restrictions, and distribution through Google Play may require review of this use case.

On Android 12+, background FGS starts need a platform exemption; a visible user action is supported, and the background-start documentation also refers to apps permitted to start activities from the background. A system-bound accessibility service can qualify under the documented system-bound-service allowance, but a later disabled service or generic background callback must not be assumed to qualify. Detach starts the monitor as part of beginning the pause, before accessibility is disabled (the bound service's allowance is the only background exemption Detach has), and re-attaches it only from a visible Detach screen, the still-bound service, or the timer (where Android usually refuses). Every denial is caught and falls back to timer/manual behavior. Acceptance of a start request is not proof that the monitor is running. Android 15's visible-overlay requirement for the `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` exemption is not used; Detach does not request that permission. Boot/update never launch this monitor. The `shortService` three-minute lifetime is inappropriate for a configurable pause up to an hour.

`specialUse` has no time limit on Android 15–17 (only `dataSync`, `mediaProcessing` and `shortService` do), so no `onTimeout` is needed; the pause deadline bounds the monitor's lifetime.

Android 12+ inexact alarms can be delivered late; Doze, OEM power management and process restrictions can add delay. Detach requests no new exact-alarm permission: it uses `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle` only where the platform already permits it (below Android 12, `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` granted, or the app on the power-save allowlist, which battery "Unrestricted" does and which `canScheduleExactAlarms()` reports), otherwise `setAndAllowWhileIdle`. AOSP's `AlarmManagerService` gives such an alarm a delivery window of 3/4 of its lead time (none under ten seconds, at most an hour): about 3.5 minutes for a 5-minute pause, as seen on the phone. `setWindow()` with a short window does not help: for apps targeting Android 12+ (`ENFORCE_MINIMUM_WINDOW_ON_INEXACT_ALARMS`), any window shorter than min(3/4 of the lead time, 10 minutes) is widened to that, and `setWindow` alarms are not allowed while idle. So each reminder alarm for the pause's end time is set at 4/7 of the remaining time, locked or not: even delivered at the very end of its window it fires by the deadline, and each delivery sets the next step, down to an alarm due within ten seconds, which has no window (about five steps for 5 minutes, seven for an hour; each is one allow-while-idle alarm, exempt from app-standby alarm quotas, and nothing else runs; Doze may still hold them while the phone is idle). Stepping only while unlocked was not enough: the usual flow is to pay and lock the phone, and a single alarm set then for the end time could still be minutes late when the phone is unlocked at that time, with nothing to set a new one. Short retries (a due timer waiting for the unlock, a payment app in front) are one unstepped alarm each. If delivery falls outside the original automatic-authority window, reconnect is refused and manual fallback remains. Restoring the reminder after a reboot does not restore authority to re-enable accessibility. The app and the notification say "Resumes by about" rather than promising a time.
